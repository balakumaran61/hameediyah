Malaysia's oldest nasi kandar, Lebuh Campbell, Penang.

In 1907 a spice trader's family carried rice and curry across a field on Campbell Street, two baskets balanced on one pole: the kandar (Malay: shoulder pole). That pole gave nasi kandar its name. The family has kept the pots full ever since.

Malaysia's oldest nasi kandar restaurant · Malaysia Book of Records, 2020

A spice trader sails for Penang.

From the Tamil Nadu coast to the Penang docks, early 1900s.

Before there was a restaurant, there was a merchant with a hold full of spices. He crossed the Bay of Bengal with his three sons and set up shop on Campbell Street.

II · The stall, 1907

Under the Angsana tree.

Campbell Street, 1907. Two baskets, one pole.

Cooked food wasn't sold in shops back then. So the family cooked behind their spice shop and carried the curries to the field across the road, two baskets on one shoulder pole: the kandar.

kandar (Malay): the shoulder pole. Nasi kandar is "pole rice", named for how it was carried.

III · The roasting ritual

Every pot starts with whole spices.

Bought whole. Roasted, ground and mixed in-house.

The masala (house spice blend) behind every Hameediyah curry still starts as whole spices. Fennel and cumin are in the founder's blend to this day. Drag a spice into the kuali (wok) and roast it slowly.

"We still buy whole spices, mix, roast and grind them ourselves."Abdul Sukkor Syed Ibrahim, seventh generation · New Straits Times, 20 Aug 2019

Meet the four signatures

The signature interaction. (1) Drag a spice from the tray into the kuali; it drops in with a little bounce and a sizzle line. (2) Press and hold (or scroll while the section is pinned) to roast: the meter fills, the spices darken from raw → toasted → roasted, and smoke wisps rise. (3) Hover or tap a spice for its one-line note. (4) At "Roasted" the finish line appears and leads into H5. Releasing the hold pauses the roast; it never burns.

V · Banjir

Let the curries flood the rice.

Ask for kuah campur (mixed gravies): a ladle of every gravy.

Pick your rice, point at your pieces, then ask for the kuah (curry gravy). Regulars ask for it mixed and poured until the rice is flooded. That's banjir (Malay: flood). Tap the pots to ladle.

Tap a pot: a ladle tips and the gravy pours in an arc onto the rice; it spreads outward and the colours blend where kuahs meet. Each pot adds a wider flood. The label names the combination. Reduced motion: the plate fills instantly, no pour.
The CTA hands off to the menu's plate builder with these kuahs already chosen (passed in the URL), so the "wow" turns straight into building a real plate. Keyboard: pots are toggle buttons (Tab, Enter/Space); the label is a live region.
